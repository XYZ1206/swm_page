DynaRep: A Visual Representation World Model for Object-Centric Dynamics

Anonymous Authors

Paper arXiv Code

TL;DR: DynaRep represents future world states in the DINO representation space rather than a pixel-reconstruction latent, and jointly models future visual representations and 3D object flow with asymmetric, modality-specific noise schedules — letting visual states stabilize earlier and continuously guide flow generation.

Abstract

Most embodied world models predict futures in latent spaces optimized for pixel reconstruction, which may not be ideal for capturing manipulation-relevant dynamics. We present DynaRep, a world model that represents future world states in a visual representation space rich in semantic and geometric structure. To this end, we jointly model future DINO representations and 3D object flow to capture object-centric dynamics. To make dense DINO features tractable for generative modeling, we introduce DINO-VAE, which performs channel and temporal compression with causal temporal modeling to produce compact latents while preserving informative visual structure. Building on this compact representation space, we employ a unified diffusion transformer with joint attention to capture the bidirectional dependencies between future visual representations and 3D object flow. To further strengthen cross-modal coupling, DynaRep adopts asymmetric modality-specific noise schedules, allowing visual representations to stabilize earlier during denoising and continuously guide 3D object flow generation. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation data show that DINO-derived representations provide a more effective space for future modeling than pixel-reconstruction-oriented latents, leading to more geometrically consistent and task-relevant 3D object flow. These results demonstrate the value of predictive visual representations for modeling object-centric dynamics in embodied interaction.

Method

01

A visual representation world model

DynaRep jointly models future visual representations and 3D object flow within a single generative framework. The representation latents and the flow tokens are concatenated and processed by one diffusion transformer, whose joint attention lets the two streams interact bidirectionally: predicted future visual states guide flow generation, while flow supplies explicit object-motion information for predicting future scene states. Future scene states and object-centric dynamics are thereby coupled rather than treated as separate prediction problems.

02

DINO-VAE: a compact, generation-friendly latent space

Dense DINO features are too high-dimensional to diffuse directly. DINO-VAE compresses them along both time and channels: attention pooling aggregates every four consecutive frames into one temporal latent, and a causal ViT encoder maps the 1024-dimensional features into a low-dimensional latent that a symmetric decoder inverts. Latent regularization — a β-VAE objective with a cosine consistency term — keeps the space smooth, so the result stays compact and generation-friendly while preserving the key visual information.

03

Modality-specific noise scheduling

The two streams do not share a diffusion timestep. Timesteps are sampled independently and mapped through shifted, modality-specific schedules, with flow biased toward higher noise so that visual representations form stable structure earlier and keep guiding motion — a latent-forcing-style asymmetric denoising trajectory. Because training sees varied visual–flow noise combinations, one model also conditions in either direction: clean future representations give flow generation (inverse dynamics), and clean flow gives future representation prediction.

What Is 3D Object Flow?

3D object flow is the motion representation DynaRep predicts: a set of query points on the manipulated object, tracked through the episode as metric 3D trajectories. The clips below render that representation directly in 3D — 64 query points drawn on the point cloud of the first frame, with each trajectory growing as the episode plays and coloured by time (cyan at the start, red at the end). The scene itself is frozen; only the trajectories move.

The trajectories shown here are generated by DynaRep. Rendering them in 3D, rather than projected onto the image, makes the metric structure of the predicted motion visible — how far the object travels, along what path, and at what height above the table.
